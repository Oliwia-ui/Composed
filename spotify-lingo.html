What if learning a language felt more like playing a game? Find a song, listen to it and guess the missing word.

01 · the idea

Music gets stuck in my head anyway. Might as well learn something from it.

Your favourite songs. A new way to learn.

Spotify-Lingo turns music into a language-learning game. Search for a song, listen to a preview and follow along with the lyrics.

One word disappears. Three possible answers appear. Pick the right one and keep playing.

The idea is simple: you're not just memorising random vocabulary. You're learning words in a context you've already heard and enjoyed.

03 · the logic

Random wrong answers make a boring game. I wanted the alternatives to actually make sense in the context of the song.

Building one round, step by step.

  1. 01 · find usable songsThe backend requests songs and checks whether lyrics are available. Songs without usable lyrics are filtered out. Users can also search by language.
  2. 02 · choose a lineA random line is selected, with at least four words so there's enough context to understand it.
  3. 03 · hide a wordOne word longer than three letters is removed. I didn't want every answer to be something obvious like "the".
  4. 04 · make believable alternativesTwo incorrect answers are taken from the same song. This makes the options feel more connected to the actual lyrics.
  5. 05 · shuffle and playAll three answers are shuffled before the round is sent to the frontend.

04 · leading the team

Being the lead developer didn't mean doing everything myself. It meant making sure everything worked together.

Four people. Different tasks. One project.

Spotify-Lingo was built by a team of four. One of my teammates created the design, while I took the lead on development.

I built the game logic and backend, oversaw the technical side and kept an eye on how everything connected.

Everyone had their own parts to build. I let the team work on those, checked what they had made and stepped in when something needed fixing or explaining.

A big part of my role was making sure we all understood how the data moved through the application. Because having four separate pieces of code isn't much use if they don't work together.

Leading isn't about controlling every detail. It's about knowing when to step in, when to let people work and how to keep everyone moving in the same direction.
