Not everything is easy to say out loud. Moodie gives young people another way to communicate what they're feeling with the people they trust.

01 · the idea

Sometimes you know something is wrong. You just don't know how to explain it to someone sitting right in front of you.

Some feelings need another way out.

Moodie is a concept for children and young people who struggle to communicate how they feel.

It gives them a space to check in, write things down and share what is happening with a trusted person, such as a parent or psychologist.

The same app has three different user experiences: one for the young person, one for their trusted person and one for the professional. Because everyone involved needs different information and different tools.

02 · the screens

I wanted it to feel like something you'd actually want to open. Not something that reminds you of an appointment with a doctor.

A little space to understand yourself.

Moodie includes a mood check-in, a personal journal and a weekly overview to help users see how their feelings change over time.

There is also an SOS button that connects the user to their trusted person or emergency services.

The aim is to make expressing emotions feel more approachable, while keeping support within reach when it matters.

03 · the name

I started with BridgeTalk. It sounded clever in my head. Then I realised it didn't actually tell anyone what the app was about.

From BridgeTalk to Moodie.

The original concept was called BridgeTalk, with a small bridge as its logo. The idea was about connecting people, but the name felt too abstract.

I wanted something more approachable, something that immediately felt connected to emotions. That's how Moodie came along.

The identity evolved too. First a bridge, then four emoji faces, and finally four soft dots representing different moods. A much simpler direction, and one that made more sense for the product.

04 · what I'd change

This is where I learned that making something fun isn't always the same as making it helpful.

I'd rethink the rewards.

Moodie includes badges, streaks and a mascot to make emotional check-ins feel lighter and more engaging.

But the more I thought about it, the more I questioned the idea of rewarding people for checking in or having a good day.

What happens when someone has a difficult week? Does breaking a streak make them feel like they've failed? Does a badge turn something personal into another task to complete?

I'd remove or rethink those features. I want people to feel that every emotion is allowed, not that some days deserve more recognition than others.

Just because a feature looks motivating doesn't mean it makes someone feel better.

05 · how it ended

Passed, with positive feedback.

A project that made me think more carefully about the emotional side of design, and about how small interface decisions can change the way someone feels.
