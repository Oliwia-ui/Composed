A little more awareness. A little less pressure. A concept for bringing calm into the everyday through a Garmin smartwatch.

01 · the problem

I kept thinking about how different breathing feels underwater. You slow down because you have to. I wondered why we wait until we're overwhelmed to do the same thing on land.

Stress doesn't always announce itself.

Sometimes it builds up quietly. Your heart rate changes, your breathing shifts and your body reacts before you really notice what's happening.

Smartwatches already collect useful information, but most of the time you see it afterwards as numbers, charts and statistics. Many mental health apps also depend on you recognising what you're feeling and actively asking for help.

But what if you're already too overwhelmed to do that?

The goal wasn't to measure more. It was to make the information useful when someone needs it.

02 · research

I expected people to like the idea of early detection. What I didn't expect was how little trust they had in the watch doing it.

22 people. A few important answers.

59%say anxiety and panic support is important or very important.
64%would likely use early stress detection and support.
50%expect the feature to fit easily into their everyday routine.
45%trust a smartwatch to detect their stress.

Online survey · n = 22 · additional interviews.

These answers shaped the project. I couldn't just design a clever feature. I also had to make people feel comfortable using it.

03 · the flow

Imagine trying to navigate five menus when you can barely focus on one thing. No. The watch needs to keep it simple.

From noticing to feeling better.

  1. 01DetectHeart rate, breathing and movement move away from your usual patterns.
  2. 02AcknowledgeOne simple button to say “I'm panicking”. Or let Sense start the support flow automatically.
  3. 03BreatheA slow visual orb and gentle vibrations guide you through breathing at 6.5 breaths per minute.
  4. 04GroundThe 5-4-3-2-1 grounding technique, divided into five small steps.
  5. 05ReturnNo grade. No pressure. Just the option to reflect afterwards.

05 · the decision I'm proudest of

The more overwhelmed someone feels, the less I want to ask them to think.

One sense. One small step.

The 5-4-3-2-1 grounding technique sounds simple. Name five things you can see, four you can touch, three you can hear and so on.

But when someone is panicking, even remembering all five steps can feel like too much.

So I broke the exercise into five individual cards. One sense, one small task, one action at a time. Complete it and move on.

No long instructions. No complicated choices. Just something simple to focus on while everything else settles.

07 · fitting into Garmin

I didn't want it to look like some random wellness app wearing a Garmin logo. It needed to belong there.

Garmin, but with a softer side.

Before designing Sense, I studied Garmin's existing brand. The website, packaging, campaigns, social media and retail experience. I wanted to understand what made Garmin recognisable before changing anything.

The existing brand is clear, technical and performance-driven. I kept that foundation, including its typography and familiar blue tones, but explored what happens when you introduce more warmth.

Softer colours, gentler shapes, calmer motion and an interface that feels less demanding.

I also created a separate design system and icon set so the concept could grow while still feeling connected to the Garmin identity.

08 · what I'd change

Funny how easy it is to forget your own rules when you start adding more features.

I would simplify the premium experience.

One of the biggest things I learned from this project is that adding more information doesn't automatically make something more helpful.

My research showed that people didn't want extra pressure or scores. Yet my premium concept slowly started introducing things like severity indexes, health scores and forecasts.

Looking back, I would rethink that part. I would keep the difficult moments free from judgement and make deeper insights something people can explore later, when they actually want to.

Sometimes the best feature is the one you decide not to add.

09 · how it ended

Passed, with positive feedback.

One semester. One concept. A lot of questions, research and decisions that changed how I think about designing for people.
